# How we write book descriptions

These rules are for anyone writing a Reads book description, human or model.
They come from the November 2026 audit (`description-audit-2026-11.md`) and
the 50-book pilot.

## What a description is for

A reader on the book page should learn three things they can't get from the
facts line (author, pages, year):
1. **What this book is**: the story, the subject, or for a textbook, the
   level, the age and what's inside.
2. **Who it's for.**
3. **What sets it apart:** how it differs from the other volumes in its
   series, from the author's other books, or from similar books.

If we can't say at least the first one truthfully, we **don't write**. A blank
description is better than a guessed one.

## Facts

- **Only facts we're sure of.** Pages, year, publisher and language come from
  the catalogue record, never from memory. Plot details, awards, film
  adaptations and the author's biography appear only when certain.
- **Don't give away endings.**
- **A shop or publisher text may be used as a source of facts** (level, age,
  what's in the box), checked against the title. Its sentences must never be
  reused or closely paraphrased.
- **If the shop text contradicts the title** (a text about another book),
  flag it. Don't write from it.

## Language and form

- **Uzbek, Latin script, plain apostrophe** (`o'`, `g'`), the way Reads
  stores text (the import normalises it anyway). "Siz" if the reader is
  addressed at all, but mostly describe the book, don't talk to the reader.
- **Length depends on what we know:**
  - one volume of a series or a textbook: 2–4 sentences (about 200–500
    characters);
  - a book we know well: 1–2 short paragraphs (up to about 900 characters).
- **Vary the openings.** Not every text starts with the title or with "Ushbu
  kitob…". Start with whatever matters most: the story, the level, the
  question the book answers.
- **The book's language:** if the edition isn't in Uzbek, end with a plain
  line such as "Kitob rus tilida." or "Kitob ingliz tilida."
- **Title in quotes** “…” the first time, author's name as Uzbek readers
  know it (Xeminguey, Tolstoy, Koelo).

## Never

- Shop and marketing clichés: *befarq qoldirmaydi, unutilmas, sehrli olam,
  ajoyib sayohat, bir nafasda o'qiladi, albatta o'qing, tavsiya etamiz,
  har bir o'quvchi uchun, eng sevimli, sizni … olamiga olib kiradi,
  o'qishni boshlasangiz to'xtay olmaysiz*.
- Their English and Russian twins: *captivating, unforgettable journey,
  must-read, delve, tapestry, testament to, увлекательное путешествие,
  захватывающий, погрузитесь*.
- Superlatives with no fact behind them ("eng yaxshi", "eng mashhur") unless
  they're verifiable (a Nobel prize, a bestseller list that is named).
- Prices, shops, delivery, "sotuvda", "chegirma", stock.
- Emoji, exclamation marks, all caps, rhetorical questions in a row.
- Spec sheets in the text (format, cover, paper): those are catalogue
  fields.
- Copying one text onto several books. Every volume gets its own words; a
  shared series sentence may appear, but the volume's own part must lead.

## Series and sets

- **Say which volume it is and what is in it:** level (A1, Starter, 3-sinf),
  age (4–5 yosh), number in the series, the stories or topics it covers if
  the title names them.
- **One short sentence of series context** at most, in our own words.
- **Readers from a course** (Oxford Family and Friends Readers, Macmillan
  readers...): name the story if it is a known tale (Sinbad, The Shoemaker
  and the Elves) and say what it retells, plus the level.
- **The same book in two bindings or scripts** (A4/A5, soft/hard, Latin and
  Cyrillic) is **one book**, not a series. Don't write; list it for merging.

## When to skip

Skip and give a one-line reason when:
- it's test prep, a workbook or a textbook and the title doesn't say what's
  inside;
- it's a local religious or academic book we don't know;
- it's a novel we don't know and the only source is the shop text;
- the record looks wrong (the title and the text describe different books).
